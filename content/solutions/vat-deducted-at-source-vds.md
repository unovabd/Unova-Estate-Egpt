Egypt's VAT rules for real estate aren't a single flat percentage — how VAT gets deposited depends on the deal, and getting it wrong on paper means your VAT return doesn't match what's actually in your books. **Unova's VDS-aware accounting** treats this as an accounting workflow, not a tax-rate dropdown.

---

## 1. VAT Recognized the Moment a Sale Is Approved

Every sale posts its VAT liability atomically at approval — alongside revenue, COGS, and the AR entry — regardless of which of the two paths below the VAT ultimately takes.

---

## 2. Two Ways VAT Actually Gets Paid

* **Company deposits VAT:** the standard path — the seller collects and deposits VAT to the NBR.
* **Customer deposits VAT directly (VDS):** the buyer deposits VAT Deducted at Source straight to the tax authority. Unova tracks this as its own liability until the customer's VDS certificate is uploaded and verified — that upload is what formally clears it.

Both paths post to the ledger correctly from day one, so your books never have to be reconciled after the fact to figure out which sales were VDS and which weren't.

---

## 3. A VAT Return That Matches Your Books

The Monthly VAT Return Summary is computed directly from posted ledger transactions — Output VAT minus VDS cleared equals net payable — instead of being recalculated from sale line items separately, which is where VAT returns and actual ledgers tend to drift apart in spreadsheet-based accounting.
